What the saveBOARD ERP does today, how its customer and sales data is organised, and where a CRM should connect. Written as the reference for the CRM specification.

Customer data

The ERP's customer is the trading account: who is billed, on what terms and prices, and whether they can order. Each entity has its own customer list; a name is unique within an entity.

Billing addressLine 1, line 2, city, region or state, postcode, country.

The original plan had a separate contacts list per customer. It wasn't built: the ERP keeps one primary contact plus a contact per delivery site. A list of people, their roles and their history is a natural job for the CRM.

Quotes, orders and invoices

A quote is a sales order in quote status, so it keeps its number (SO-1594, for example) when it becomes an order. Numbers continue from Katana, separately for each entity.

Quote statuses

Draft→Sent→Accepted (converted to an order)orDeclinedorExpired

Sales order statuses

Open→Picked→Shipped→Invoiced→Closed (paid in Xero)orCancelled
  • Invoicing before shipping: an open or picked order can be invoiced up front (cash, COD, or custom work paid before production). It keeps its status, can't be edited until the invoice is undone, and becomes Invoiced when fully shipped.
  • One order, one invoice. Invoices are numbered with the order number and created in Xero as drafts for approval. A paid invoice closes its order.
  • Returns are numbered RET-… against a shipped order. Their credit notes go to Xero with the same number.
  • Stock on each order line shows In stock or Not available, with a Make button that starts a manufacturing order.
  • Sales history covers Katana orders from November 2021 (NZ) and February 2023 (AUS) onward, with sales by customer and by product.

Rules a CRM must respect

  • Two separate companies. A customer trading with both NZ and AUS is two customer records, one in each entity, with separate Xero contacts.
  • Prices and stock come from the ERP. Quote prices come from the customer's price list, then the default list, then the last price charged. Availability is calculated from the stock ledger.
  • Exports are zero-rated. Goods shipped outside the entity's country carry 0% GST; freight still carries GST.
  • Credit hold stops new sales orders but still allows quotes.
  • Cost and margin never leave the business. They never appear on quotes, packing slips or work orders, and shouldn't appear in anything shared with customers.
  • Xero holds the money. The ERP pushes invoices and credit notes and reads payment status back. It never takes payments itself.

How the two can connect

  1. Today, no development: Excel exports from Customers, Quotes, Sales orders, Invoicing and Insights → Sales by customer, imported into the CRM. Good for a pilot.
  2. Recommended next step: a small read-only ERP API (customers, quotes and orders, invoice status, sales by customer) that the CRM calls with its own key. It runs per entity and shares no passwords.
  3. Later, if needed: notifications from the ERP to the CRM when something happens: quote sent, quote accepted, order shipped, invoice paid. Optionally, a "create customer" call from the CRM when a prospect wins, which the ERP checks against its rules (unique name, entity).

Matching records: link CRM accounts to ERP customers by the ERP's customer ID plus entity (NZ or AUS), not by name. Names change and can differ between systems. Quotes and orders are best referenced by their SO number, which staff and customers already use.

Open items

  • AUS stocktake (week of 5 October) and AUS recipes from Katana's manufacturing ingredients export.
  • Standard costs for raw materials. Many are $0 from Katana, which understates manufacturing costs and margins.
  • Company details (address, GST/ABN, bank) on quote and order PDFs.
  • CRM link: confirm the division of work above, then build the read-only API.
  • Possible later: single sign-on, more than one contact per customer (if the CRM doesn't hold people), staging copy for testing.
